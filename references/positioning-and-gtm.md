# Positioning, Messaging & Go-to-Market

Positioning is the foundation everything else rests on. Get it wrong and great execution just helps the wrong people find a product they won't buy. Get it right and channels, copy, and pricing fall into place.

---

## Positioning

Positioning answers: **for whom, in what category, is this product the obvious best choice, and why?**

### Positioning structure

| Component | Question it answers | Example (link-in-bio tool) |
|---|---|---|
| **Target customer** | Who specifically gets the most value? (narrow is stronger early) | Indonesian creators and UMKM who need a free link page |
| **Category / frame of reference** | What does the user mentally file this under? | Link-in-bio tool / microsite builder |
| **Key differentiated value** | What does it do better/differently that the target actually cares about? | Fastest to publish, works in Bahasa, priced for local reality |
| **Proof** | Why is that claim believable? | Template library ready in 60 seconds; Indonesian-language interface; free tier with no credit card |

**Test a positioning statement:** Would the target customer nod immediately? Would a competitor be unable to claim the same thing with a straight face? If yes to both — it's sharp.

## Messaging

Messaging translates positioning into words on the page.

### Messaging hierarchy

1. **Primary message / value prop** — one line, benefit-led, in the customer's language
   - ✅ "Publish your link page in 60 seconds, for free"
   - ❌ "Microsite platform with templating engine and analytics"

2. **Supporting messages** — the 3 reasons that line is true, each with proof
   - "Works in Bahasa Indonesia" → screenshot of UI
   - "No credit card required" → zero-friction sign-up flow
   - "Your links, not ours — no redirects, no branding unless you want it"

3. **Per-segment variants** — same core value, reframed for different buyers
   - Creator: "One link for all your content"
   - UMKM: "One page for your whole business"
   - Agency: "White-label link pages for all your clients"

### Messaging principles
- Lead with **outcome over feature** ("get more clicks" not "advanced analytics")
- Use **the customer's words** — mine reviews, support tickets, social comments for exact phrasing
- **One primary CTA** per surface
- **Specificity beats adjectives** — "3 clicks" beats "easy"; "published in 60 seconds" beats "fast"

## Competitive differentiation

When positioning against a competitor:

### Process
1. **Map the landscape honestly** — what each competitor is genuinely good at and known for. Don't strawman; buyers know the truth.

2. **Find the wedge** — where the target customer is underserved by the leader. Often it's:
   - A segment the leader ignores (language, price point, region, workflow)
   - A specific use case they handle poorly
   - A pricing tier gap

3. **Pick a position you can own and defend:**
   - Cheaper-and-good
   - Specialized-for-segment-X
   - Local/localized (language, payment methods, cultural context)
   - Integrated-with-Y (specific ecosystem)
   - Avoid "we have more features" — it's unwinnable and uncompelling

4. **Translate to marketing surface:**
   - `/alternatives/[competitor]` comparison page (strong for SEO + intent capture)
   - "Why [product] vs [competitor]" page
   - Copy that names the buyer's real frustration with the incumbent without attacking directly

### Localization as a differentiation wedge
If the product operates in a specific market (language, region), this is often the sharpest wedge:
- "Built for [market], in [language], priced for [local reality]"
- A global competitor's pricing in USD feels expensive to UMKM in Indonesia; local pricing is a feature
- Local payment methods (GoPay, DANA, OVO vs credit card) can be a conversion advantage

## Go-to-Market (launching a product or feature)

A launch is a coordinated moment, not a single email. Scale the ceremony to the thing: a small feature gets a changelog post; a major product gets the full sequence.

### GTM checklist

| Step | What to do |
|---|---|
| 1. Lock positioning & messaging | Who it's for, one-line value, proof. Everything downstream uses this. |
| 2. Choose audience & channels | Where the target actually is; pick 2–3 channels matched to motion |
| 3. Prepare assets | Landing page, announcement copy, email to existing users, social, demo/visual, docs |
| 4. Sequence & timing | Tease (optional) → Launch moment (coordinate channels same day) → Sustain (follow-up, objections, early wins) |
| 5. Define success metric upfront | Sign-ups, activations, upgrades, or feature adoption. Decide before launch what "it worked" means. |
| 6. Close the feedback loop | Capture reactions and objections; feed back into messaging within days |

### GTM plan output format

When asked for a GTM plan, deliver:
1. **Goal + success metric** (the one number that proves it worked)
2. **Positioning one-liner** (for the launch)
3. **Audience + 2–3 channels** (ranked, with rationale)
4. **Asset list** (what needs to be created; offer to draft key ones)
5. **Timed sequence** (e.g., Day -7 teaser → Day 0 launch → Day +3 follow-up → Day +14 roundup)
6. **What you'll measure weekly**

Then offer to draft the key assets (landing page outline, announcement, launch email) as the next step.
